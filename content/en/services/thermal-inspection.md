Using the infrared camera aboard our **DJI Matrice 4TD**, we detect in a matter of hours what the naked eye can never see: heat loss, water infiltration, faulty photovoltaic cells, electrical hot spots. Drone thermal inspection eliminates the need for scaffolding and reduces intervention time by 70–90% compared to traditional methods.

## Why choose drone thermal inspection?

Traditional thermal audits require scaffolding, aerial platforms or rope access — time-consuming, expensive and sometimes impossible on certain structures. Drones remove these constraints:

- **Speed**: a 2,000 m² roof inspected in under one hour
- **Safety**: no work at height for your teams or contractors
- **Precision**: 640 × 512 px thermal sensor with super-resolution up to 1,280 × 1,024 px, sensitivity < 30 mK
- **Traceability**: illustrated report with georeferenced anomaly mapping and paired IR + visible images

## Concrete applications

### Roofs and building envelopes
Thermal bridges, insulation defects, moisture infiltration under waterproof membranes — infrared pinpoints heat loss zones that standard energy audits cannot localise precisely. Ideal before energy renovation works to target interventions efficiently.

### Photovoltaic panels
On ground-mounted or rooftop solar installations, we identify cell by cell any hot spots (failed cells, partial shading, delamination, faulty connections) that penalise your system's output.

### Agricultural and industrial buildings
Farm buildings, greenhouses, warehouses: thermal inspection prioritises renovation works before winter and reduces energy bills. It also detects moisture infiltration in metal sheet roofing before it damages stored goods.

### Electrical networks and technical equipment
Detection of hot spots on outdoor electrical cabinets, cables, connections and transformers — before overheating causes downtime or fire. The thermal camera operates at night without disrupting activity.

## Who is it for?

- **Local authorities**: schools, town halls, sports halls, social housing — comply with mandatory energy audits
- **Farmers and livestock farmers**: farm buildings, greenhouses
- **Solar farm operators**: performance monitoring, early fault detection
- **Property managers and housing associations**: planned maintenance before renovation
- **Energy consultants**: input data for regulatory audits
- **Insurers**: expert appraisal following moisture or fire damage

## When to intervene?

Thermal inspection is optimal when the temperature difference between inside and outside exceeds **10 °C**. Best intervention windows:

- **Autumn and winter** (October to March) for heated buildings
- **Early morning or late afternoon** to minimise parasitic solar radiation on rooftops
- **At night** for electrical equipment and cooled solar panels
